# Safe sharing

Not everything is off-limits in AI. You can use AI safely at work if you share the right things and keep the wrong things out. This section shows you how to tell the difference, step by step.

## 1. What is usually okay to share

These are generally low-risk things to put into an AI chat:

- Public information anyone could find (holiday schedules, public policies already published)
- Generic, made-up examples with no real names or details
- Your own thoughts and questions that contain no confidential information
- Help with writing, formatting, or brainstorming where you supply the content yourself
- Code snippets or text that do not include customer data, secrets, or personal information

Even for these, remember: if you're on a work device or network, your employer may still see what you type.

## 2. What to keep out

Never share:

- Patient or health information (see [Never paste patient or payroll info](never-paste-patient-payroll.md))
- Payroll, salaries, Social Security numbers, or bank details
- Passwords, login credentials, or security codes
- Customer lists, client contact details, or contracts
- Trade secrets, pricing, or unreleased plans
- Anything under a nondisclosure agreement

## 3. De-identify before you discuss

If you must discuss a real work situation, strip out everything that could identify a person:

| Remove | Replace with |
|--------|--------------|
| Names | "the patient," "the employee," "the customer" |
| Specific dates | "last month," "recently" |
| Exact ages | broad ranges ("in their 60s") |
| Towns, clinics, departments | generic terms ("a rural clinic," "my department") |
| Rare conditions or unique details | common, general versions |

If someone could still figure out who you're talking about, don't paste it.

## 4. Use approved tools

If your employer provides an approved AI tool, prefer it over personal accounts for work tasks — and still follow the rules in this guide. Approved does not mean "safe for patient data." Check what your employer's tool allows before you paste anything.

## 5. Keep work and personal separate

- Don't log into personal AI accounts on work devices if your employer prohibits it
- Don't paste personal sensitive information (your own medical info, bank details, passwords) into work AI tools
- Use different browsers or profiles for work and personal if it helps you keep them straight

## 6. When in doubt, don't paste it

If you're asking yourself "Is this okay to paste?", the answer is to wait and check:

- Look at your employer's AI or data policy
- Ask IT, compliance, or your supervisor
- Use a made-up example instead

Five minutes of checking beats months of cleanup after a leak.

## Quick checklist for this section

- [ ] I only share public info, made-up examples, or my own non-confidential thoughts in AI chats
- [ ] I de-identify any real situation before discussing it with AI
- [ ] I use my employer's approved AI tools where available
- [ ] I keep work and personal AI accounts separate
- [ ] When I'm unsure, I ask before I paste
