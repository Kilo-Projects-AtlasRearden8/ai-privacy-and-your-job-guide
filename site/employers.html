What employers can see

The short version: if you're using AI on a work device, work network, or a work-provided AI tool, your employer can often see what you're doing — including what you type into AI chats.

1. Company devices and networks

If you use a laptop, phone, or tablet your employer gave you, your employer may install software that can see:

  • Which websites you visit, including AI tool websites
  • What you type into forms and chat boxes, depending on the monitoring software
  • Files you open, edit, or upload

Even on your personal device, if you connect to your employer's Wi-Fi, your employer's network team may see which AI sites you visit — though usually not the exact words you type, if the site uses HTTPS (the lock icon in your browser).

Rule of thumb: assume anything you do on a work device or work network can be seen by your employer.

2. Work-provided AI tools

Many employers provide AI through enterprise accounts — for example Microsoft 365 Copilot, Google Workspace Gemini, or similar. With these tools:

  • Your chats are usually tied to your work email account
  • Your employer's admin may be able to review usage logs or chat history
  • The data may be stored on your employer's cloud account, not a personal one
  • Your employer may have a contract saying your data is not used to train the AI model — but you should confirm this with IT, not assume it

Before you use a work-provided AI tool, ask your IT or compliance team:

  • Is this tool approved for work use?
  • Can my admin see my chat history?
  • Is my data used to train the model?
  • What types of data are allowed in this tool?

3. Personal AI accounts used at work

If you log into a personal ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or similar account on your own device at work:

  • Your employer generally cannot see the contents of those chats through the AI company
  • But if you're on a work device or work network, monitoring software on your device or network may still see that you're visiting the AI site
  • If you paste work information into a personal account, that information leaves your employer's control entirely

4. What "can see" means in practice

Employers usually don't read every employee's AI chats one by one. What happens more often:

  • Automated monitoring flags certain keywords or sites
  • IT reviews logs after a security incident or audit
  • A manager sees something because you shared your screen in a meeting

You don't need to be paranoid. You just need to be intentional: don't put anything in an AI chat that you wouldn't want your employer to read.

Quick checklist for this section

  • I know whether my device is personal or employer-provided
  • I know whether my employer provides an approved AI tool
  • I have asked IT or compliance whether my chats can be seen
  • I assume anything on a work device or network can be monitored
